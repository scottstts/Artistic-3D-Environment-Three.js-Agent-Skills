# Designing when the reference is silent or dropped

## When this applies

- Areas the reference doesn't show: behind the camera, the world beyond the frame, interiors, the far side of a structure.
- Detail the reference can't inform: a zoomed-out shot, or AI noise where a motif should be.
- The user drops the reference, or asks you to design.

## Method

1. **State the theme in one line.** What place is this, and what is it for? Everything invented must be plausible for that place.
2. **List five ideas fast, then set them aside.** They are the textbook answers: a tile grid, an urn, a plain box opening, a generic cloud bank, a ring of columns. Build something more original that is still coherent with the theme: a form particular to this place, its purpose and its materials, rather than the stock element of its type.
3. **Design from function and construction.**
   - Where does the light enter?
   - What carries the load?
   - How would a skilled maker build and decorate this, with real joints and real materials?
   - Design choices that solve a real problem of the place (how light reaches a dark space, how a span is carried) read as intentional.
4. **Give it a hierarchy:** one dominant gesture, a supporting rhythm, then fine detail. Ornament is composed: continuous, growing from the structure. Never a scatter.
5. **Keep its character continuous with the reference.** Use its palette, material families and level of detail. Invented areas must not read as a different project.
6. **Judge the scene on its own terms:** as a real, well-designed place, from every angle.

## Taste checks

- Does it have identity, or could it be any building of that type?
- Is anything there just to fill space or raise density? Remove it.
- Does every opening, light patch and shadow have a physical cause in the design? Light spots on a floor need holes that make them.
- Is the repetition designed (rhythm, alternation, variation) or merely copied?
- If you showed a skilled architect, gardener or set designer, would they recognize the decisions as theirs?
