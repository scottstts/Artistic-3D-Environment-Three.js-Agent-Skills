# What "done" means

A scene is done when all four hold.

## 1. It matches the reference

From the reference viewpoint:

- **Structure:** composition, orientation, proportions, counts and scale match what the reference shows.
- **Light:** the value structure matches: where light and shade fall, the key-to-fill ratio, the amount of haze and the strength of bloom.
- **Region measurements:** they agree with the reference within the noise of the method. Where they don't, the difference is deliberate and stated: true shapes kept over a deformed frame, or an AI-generated impossibility replaced with the plausible version.

With no reference, or after the user drops it, replace this condition with: it reads as real footage of a real, well-designed place.

## 2. It holds as a world

- Orbiting anywhere shows a complete, coherent place out to the horizon.
- Up close, every junction is resolved, every surface is real material, and every plant and rock is the real thing.
- No shortcut that only works from the hero angle.

## 3. It holds in motion

- No noise, shimmer, popping, trails, stepped updates or artifacts that follow the camera.
- Animated things move plausibly and never interpenetrate.

## 4. It meets the standard

Every owning skill's Standard section is met.

## Things that are not "done"

- A list of open items at the end of a report.
- "Much closer, but not a match yet."
- A metric matched while the image still looks different. The metric is blind to the gap: change the metric.
- A still that looks right while motion looks wrong.
- Your own captures showing a structural deviation you haven't fixed or flagged.
