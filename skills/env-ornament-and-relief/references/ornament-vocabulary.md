# Ornament vocabulary

Compositions first, then the motifs they are made of. For each one: what it is, how it is laid out, and how to build it.

## Compositions

| Composition | Layout | Where it goes | Build |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running scroll (rinceau) | An undulating stem. A scroll leaves each wave and coils into the bay, ending in a flower or rosette. Sheath leaves cover the junctions. | Friezes, bands, borders, rails | Carved height field on the band's ground, or swept stems with modelled leaves for high relief |
| Mirrored scroll | Two running scrolls springing from a central rosette, vase or mask, mirrored | Panels, overdoors, lintels | Build one half, then mirror (meshkit flips winding) |
| Candelabrum | A vertical axis of stacked vase, baluster and foliage elements, with scrolls branching symmetrically | Tall panels, pilaster faces | An axial stack of lathed elements, plus mirrored scroll pairs |
| Cartouche | A framed field (oval or shield) with a rolled, scrolled or strapwork border, often under a crown or shell | Keystones, overdoors, the centre of attics | A lofted or swept rolled border around a sunk or convex field, with a crest above |
| Acanthus cluster | Leaves bursting from a point, curling outward, overlapping | Capitals, brackets, junctions, the base of scrolls | Modelled leaves (cupped blades with lobes and a midrib), arranged in whorls or fans |
| Frieze of repeats (dentils, egg-and-dart, bead-and-reel, guilloche) | A strict rhythm along a moulding | Cornices, architraves, frames | Instanced or swept elements on the moulding. The rhythm is fitted to the length so it ends cleanly at the corners. |
| Strapwork and interlace | Flat bands crossing over and under, with real depth at the crossings | Ceilings, panels, vaults | Swept bands with an over/under offset at each crossing |
| Coffers | A grid of sunk panels with stepped frames, often with rosettes | Vaults, ceilings, domes | Panels cut into the shell, stepped frame mouldings, rosettes seated in them |
| Trophies, garlands, swags | Hanging masses of foliage, ribbons or objects from fixed points | Spandrels, friezes | Lofted swag bodies with modelled leaves along them, seated at their hangers |

## Motifs

| Motif | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scroll / volute | A spiral whose curvature increases toward the eye. Its section tapers from stem to tip. | Use an Euler or log spiral: the scroll opens wide, then closes into a tight eye. Constant-radius circles read as mechanical. |
| Acanthus leaf | A cupped blade with a midrib, 3–5 lobed fingers per side, the tip curling over | Never a flat card. The lobes overlap. |
| Rosette | A boss with 5–8 cupped petals | Sits in eyes, coffers, centres |
| Shell | Ribbed fan radiating from a hinge, with a scalloped edge | Niche heads, keystones |
| Bead | A row of spheres or ellipsoids, seated in a groove | Fine tier. Count fitted to length. |
| Egg-and-dart | Ovolo eggs with darts between them, on an ovolo moulding | The rhythm ends at the corners |
| Gadroon / flute | Convex or concave ribs on a curved body | Follows the host's curvature |

## Gilding and colour

Where the reference shows white stone with gilt, the colour follows the carving: gilt on raised members, beads and rims, white in the grounds, or the reverse. Read which. Gilt edges on white scrolls (a fine gold bead on both rims) are typical of baroque stucco.
