# Sand and snow

## Dunes

- **Scales:**
  - draa or megadunes (hundreds of metres to kilometres);
  - dunes (tens to hundreds of metres), with sharp, winding crests;
  - ripples (centimetres to decimetres), close up.
- **Anatomy:** a gentle windward slope (about 5–15°) up to a sharp crest, then a slip face on the lee side at about 30–34°. Crest lines wind and branch. Barchans are crescent-shaped, with their horns pointing downwind.
- **Lighting:** the slip faces and the windward faces take very different light. Bake self-shadowing (dune on dune) into a map if the sun is static. Sharp crests catch a bright rim.
- **Mesh:** the silhouettes of distant crests need enough mesh density. Shading can come from a baked normal map, but the silhouette is geometry.
- **Material:** scanned sand with fine grain normal and roughness, plus a ripple normal oriented across the wind. Pale limestone boulders or scrub, where the reference shows them.

## Snow

- **Deposition:** on up-facing surfaces, by slope (none on faces steeper than about 50–60°). More in sheltered spots, less on wind-scoured ridges. Drifts in the lee of objects.
- **Caps on objects:** real thickness on the top surfaces (rails, branches, roofs), rounded edges, a slight overhang on the lee side.
- **Material:** high albedo (about 0.8–0.9), bluish in shade from sky light, and fine sparkle only as real sub-pixel glints, never as noise. Subsurface softness at the edges.
- **Transitions:** snow thins to patches on rock and grass edges, rather than a hard line.
