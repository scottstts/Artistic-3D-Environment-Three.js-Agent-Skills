# Breaking waves and surf

## Form

- **A body of water,** not a sheet. In cross-section:
  - the thick base: deep, dark, absorbing;
  - the face: steep, textured by smaller waves running up it;
  - the crest: thin, so light passes through as glowing cyan or green when backlit;
  - the lip: thrown forward.
- **The face carries texture:** flow lines and small ripples following the curl. Never a smooth gradient.
- **A ridable or giant wave seen from above** reads by its overall profile. If the viewer should see the whole run down to the sea, the path must be convex: steep at the top, flattening toward the bottom. A concave profile hides the lower run.

## Life cycle

1. **Form:** the swell steepens.
2. **Throw:** the lip pitches forward.
3. **Crash:** the curl comes down to its base, fast (well under a second for small waves).
4. **Subside:** the whitewater bore flattens within about a second or two, and the surface returns to near-flat water.
5. **Rise:** the next swell builds from the flattened water.

A crashed shape lingering as an arch is a defect.

## Spray and foam

- Spray is particles or volume with real motion: ejected at the lip, falling under gravity, motion-blurred.
- Whitewater is a turbulent foam body: bright, rough, translucent at its thin edges, decaying into lace.
- Spray never covers for a weak wave body. If the wave only reads with spray on top, the body is wrong.

## Looping a wave scene

For an endless surf or wave loop, make it periodic in the wave field, not with seams. Where procedural stations wrap, every lookup (shape, texture, foam, shadow) must wrap identically. Otherwise a seam flickers as the wrap passes.
